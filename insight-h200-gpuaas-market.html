NVIDIA H200 GPU Clusters & GPUaaS: the sourcing guide for 2026.

The H200 SXM5 is the memory-bandwidth upgrade that enterprise inference deployments have been waiting for — 141 GB HBM3e versus 80 GB HBM2e, at a cost basis meaningfully below Blackwell. Here is how procurement actually works in 2026.

Published October 22, 2026

The H200 position

The H200 SXM5 is the memory-bandwidth upgrade to the H100: 141 GB HBM3e versus 80 GB HBM2e, with 4.8 TB/s memory bandwidth — a 43% VRAM increase and a 1.43× bandwidth gain over the H100. For large language model inference, where model weights must reside in GPU memory, the H200 removes the memory ceiling that forces H100 clusters to shard aggressively across more nodes. It occupies the middle tier of the current GPU hierarchy: more accessible in cost and lead time than B200 or B300, meaningfully better than H100 for inference-heavy workloads and large-model fine-tuning.

Allocation vs GPUaaS

Enterprise buyers accessing H200 capacity have two primary structures. Bare-metal allocation provides dedicated hardware at a reserved term with a fixed rate — full control over hardware locality, network fabric topology and software stack. GPU-as-a-Service provides metered access (shared or dedicated) provisioned through an operator's API, with faster time-to-compute but typically at a premium rate and with less control over fabric and interconnect. For training workloads requiring tight node-to-node communication, bare-metal allocation with InfiniBand interconnect remains the correct structure. GPUaaS suits inference serving, fine-tuning and development phases where on-demand burst matters more than interconnect control. See the GPU clusters & GPUaaS mandate for how we structure both.

The gap between a published GPUaaS rate and a negotiated bare-metal reserved rate for equivalent H200 compute is material. Buyers who take the first quote offered leave meaningful value on the table.

Configurations

DGX H200 system: 8× H200 SXM5 per node, 1,128 GB aggregate VRAM per node. NVLink 4.0 within-node interconnect (900 GB/s bidirectional per GPU pair). Rack-scale deployment: HGX H200 server boards with 3.2 Tb/s InfiniBand fabric across nodes for distributed training. Power: approximately 10.2 kW per node — same infrastructure class as H100, no liquid cooling required at the node level. Minimum enterprise cluster: 8 nodes (64 GPUs, 9,024 GB aggregate VRAM across the cluster).

The procurement window

H200 allocation sits in an unusual position in 2026: H100 inventory is increasingly surplus as operators transition to Blackwell, while Blackwell itself (B200 and B300) remains constrained and commands a premium. H200 occupies the window between the two — more capable than the surplus H100 market, more accessible than the constrained Blackwell market. This creates a procurement window where buyers who move now can secure committed H200 positions ahead of re-pricing cycles driven by Blackwell availability normalising.
