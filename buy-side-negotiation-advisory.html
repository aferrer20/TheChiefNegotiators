01

Three ways we work, and you pick

  • —
    Advise from behind

    Your team runs the negotiation and the counterparty never knows we are involved. We brief before every session, review every draft, and tell you what to hold and what to trade. The most common mode, and the most discreet.

  • —
    Lead alongside

    We are in the room as part of your team. Useful when the technical detail is dense or the relationship needs someone who can be firm without your team having to be.

  • —
    Run the table

    We negotiate as your mandated representative. Appropriate on large mandates, on anonymous approaches, or where you need a layer between you and the outcome.

02

Where buy-side leverage is usually lost

Almost always before the negotiation formally begins.

  • Timeline pressure you created. A board date, a launch commitment or a lapsing contract handed to the counterparty as information. The moment they know your deadline, the deadline is theirs.
  • Single-source drift. The technical evaluation narrows to one vendor before commercial terms are discussed, and the alternative is no longer credible. Keeping a genuine second option alive is worth more than any clause.
  • Incumbent renewal inertia. The most expensive contract in most organisations is the one nobody tested. Renewals are negotiated against last year's paper rather than this year's market.
  • No benchmark. Without knowing what comparable buyers are paying, the first number becomes the anchor and every subsequent concession feels like a win.
  • Anchoring on price alone. Term length, ramp, SLA remedies, escalators, expansion rights and exit routinely carry more value than the headline rate, and are conceded to protect it.
  • A circulated requirement. If your need has been broadcast — an open RFP, a wide search, a talkative advisor — every counterparty knows the shape of the field. This is the leak we are structured to avoid.
03

What discretion actually means here

Nothing about your requirement is circulated in order to find counterparties. We approach them directly, one at a time, and only those you approve learn anything specific. First contact can be made anonymously, an MNDA is available on request, and a mandate can run to completion without the wider market learning you were ever in it.

For buyers in a competitive market, that is often worth more than any single term in the contract.

Questions

Asked on this mandate.

01

Does the counterparty have to know you are involved?

+

No. In our most common mode, they never do. You decide how visible we are, and that decision is reversible at any point in the mandate.

02

What kinds of contracts do you work on?

+

Enterprise infrastructure and technology agreements — colocation and capacity, GPU and hardware supply, GPUaaS and cloud commitments, power and energy supply, and the master agreements underneath them.

03

How is this different from a procurement consultancy?

+

Process consultancies improve how you buy. We work the specific deal in front of you, with the market knowledge and the counterparty relationships to know what is actually achievable on it. And we cost the buyer nothing.

04

You are paid by the ecosystem. How is that not a conflict?

+

It is the fair question. The answer is structural: we hold no inventory and take no position in your deal, so nothing we put in front of you is something we need you to take. We are only useful to the ecosystem for as long as buyers keep trusting us, which makes protecting that trust the whole job.

05

We only have one viable vendor. Is there anything to negotiate?

+

Usually more than you think, and often the first thing worth testing is whether the alternative is genuinely non-viable or merely unfamiliar. Even in a true single-source position, term structure, remedies and exit remain open.
