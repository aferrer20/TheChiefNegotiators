Why It Matters On A GPU Floor

Priced the silicon carefully. Inherited the licensing.

If you rent compute, gross margin is the spread between what the hardware costs and what the capacity sells for. Microsoft licensing sits inside that spread, and it is the one line in it that scales with core count — the same number your GPU economics are built on.

Per-core cost scales with the thing that earns

Dense, high-core hosts are the whole point of a GPU or AI cluster. Windows Server and SQL Server are licensed against those same physical cores, so the hardware decision driving your revenue drives your largest software line at the same time. Edition choice and virtual-machine density modelling belong at design time, not after the first invoice.

Renting capacity changes the terms

Running your own workload and running someone else's are two different licensing positions. Provider programs, outsourcing rules and dedicated-host requirements all turn on that distinction, and the switch flips commercially — the day you sign a tenant, not the day you deploy a server.

Enterprise tenants audit before they sign

Ask anyone who has chased a regulated logo. The security questionnaire arrives before the contract, and a framework mapping you cannot produce becomes a deal you cannot close. NIST, CMMC, HIPAA and FedRAMP evidence is a gate on revenue, not a compliance chore.

It is negotiable. Most treat it as fixed.

Core counts, editions, programs, entitlements and renewal timing are all levers. Pulled together and early they move the number; pulled late, or one at a time, they do not. Negotiation is the whole of what this firm does, and on this desk it costs you nothing.

This desk sits alongside the rest of the practice. Where a licensing position is being set at the same time as a cluster procurement, a GPUaaS contract or a capacity deal, the two constrain each other and are better run together.

Glossary

The terms that decide what you owe.

Microsoft licensing has its own vocabulary, and the gap between two of these terms is usually where the money is.

SPLA · Services Provider License Agreement

The program for organisations making licensed software available to third parties as a hosted or managed service, billed monthly on actual usage rather than purchased up front. If someone else's workload runs on hardware you own or control, some form of provider licensing almost certainly applies.

CSP · Cloud Solution Provider

The partner channel for Microsoft subscription products such as Microsoft 365 and Azure. A Tier 1 direct CSP holds the billing relationship with Microsoft itself, which is what lets it commit to its own response targets, consolidate the bill and run licence administration on your behalf.

Volume licensing & the EA

The route through which an organisation licenses software for its own use, committed up front over a term. It is the wrong instrument for tenant workloads, and using it that way is the most common gap we hear about in hosting environments.

Software Assurance

An add-on to volume licences carrying upgrade rights and unlocking entitlements such as License Mobility and Azure Hybrid Benefit. Whether yours is active is often the deciding factor in what you can legally do with a licence you already own.

License Mobility

The entitlement that lets eligible server licences with active Software Assurance be deployed on a provider's shared infrastructure. It is the mechanism behind most bring-your-own-licence arrangements, and it has conditions on both sides worth checking before you rely on it.

Azure Hybrid Benefit

Applies existing Windows Server and SQL Server licences with active Software Assurance toward the cost of equivalent Azure resources, so the same workload is not paid for twice mid-migration. Most operators either do not claim it or claim it incorrectly.

Per-core licensing

Windows Server and SQL Server are licensed against the physical cores in the host, with minimums per processor and per server. The dense, high-core hosts that make GPU and AI infrastructure economic therefore carry the largest per-core licensing cost on the floor.

Outsourcing rules

The terms governing software running on infrastructure you do not own, or someone else's workload running on yours. They are why a colocation, hosting or capacity-rental arrangement can change your licensing position without a single new server being deployed.

Dedicated host

Physical hardware assigned to one customer rather than shared across tenants. Dedicated and multi-tenant models carry different licensing requirements, so the commercial shape of a deal often decides its licensing shape.

SAM engagement

A structured review, sometimes Microsoft-led and sometimes partner-led, of what you have deployed against what you are entitled to. A defensible core count and entitlement position going in is worth considerably more than a scramble once the letter arrives.

02

Can we use our existing volume licences for customer workloads on our hardware?

+

Usually not without additional rights. Volume and Enterprise Agreement licences are bought for an organisation's own use, and the moment a third party's workload runs on hardware you own or control, provider licensing terms generally apply instead.

It is the most common compliance gap in hosting and neocloud environments, and one of the quietest, because nothing appears to go wrong until somebody checks. The partner reviews how your capacity is actually consumed and tells you which program each workload should sit under.

03

How does Microsoft licensing work for a neocloud or GPU cloud provider?

+

Two things drive it. First, Windows Server and SQL Server are licensed per physical core, so the dense high-core hosts that make GPU and AI infrastructure economic also make per-core licensing one of the largest software lines on the P&L. Edition choice and virtual-machine density modelling matter enormously.

Second, the moment you rent capacity to a third party you move from standard licensing into provider territory, where SPLA, outsourcing rules and dedicated-host requirements apply. Both need modelling together, because optimising one while ignoring the other is how operators end up carrying an exposure they never priced.

04

What is the difference between SPLA and CSP?

+

They solve different problems. SPLA is the program for making licensed software available to third parties as a hosted or managed service, billed monthly on actual usage. CSP is the partner channel for Microsoft subscription products such as Microsoft 365 and Azure, bought and managed through a partner rather than direct.

Plenty of operators need both — SPLA for what they make available to tenants, CSP for the subscriptions their own business runs on. The exposure usually sits at the boundary between them.

05

How is Windows Server and SQL Server licensed in a data center?

+

Both are licensed per physical core, with minimums per processor and per server, and the edition you choose changes how much virtualisation you are entitled to run. Standard and Datacenter behave very differently at high VM density, and SQL Server layers Enterprise versus Standard feature and core considerations on top.

Getting the model wrong is the most expensive licensing mistake on a data center floor. The partner reviews real core counts, host density and workload profile — including SQL Server health checks, performance tuning and high-availability design — before recommending anything.

06

Do we still need provider licensing if our tenants bring their own licences?

+

Sometimes, and it depends on the product and on what your tenants are actually entitled to. License Mobility through Software Assurance is the mechanism that lets eligible server licences be deployed on a provider's shared infrastructure, and it carries conditions on both sides.

A bring-your-own-licence arrangement nobody has verified is a risk you are carrying on your tenant's behalf. The partner checks what can legitimately be moved and what that leaves you responsible for.

07

What is Azure Hybrid Benefit and do we qualify?

+

It lets organisations apply existing Windows Server and SQL Server licences with active Software Assurance toward the cost of equivalent Azure resources, so you are not paying twice for the same workload mid-migration. Whether you qualify depends on licence type, Software Assurance status and where the workload runs. The partner reviews your entitlements and tells you plainly what you can and cannot apply.

08

Can you help us pass a licensing audit or a customer compliance review?

+

Yes, and it is one of the most common reasons operators get in touch. Licensing optimisation establishes a defensible position on what you own and what you owe. Separately, compliance gap analysis maps your Microsoft environment to NIST, CMMC, HIPAA and FedRAMP, producing a prioritised remediation plan, configured policies that close each gap, and audit-ready documentation you can hand to a customer or an auditor.

09

How is Microsoft software licensed on a dedicated host for one customer?

+

Dedicated single-tenant hardware and shared multi-tenant infrastructure carry different licensing requirements, so the commercial shape of a deal often decides its licensing shape. Because dedicated arrangements are negotiated one at a time, the licensing position gets set per contract rather than per platform, which is how an operator ends up with several different positions across one floor. The partner reviews the arrangements you have signed and shows you where they diverge.
