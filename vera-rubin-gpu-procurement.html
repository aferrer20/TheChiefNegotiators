01

What Vera Rubin is

Vera Rubin is NVIDIA's next-generation AI computing platform — the successor to Blackwell Ultra. The name refers to two components: the Rubin GPU architecture (NVR100), and the Vera CPU, an ARM-based processor that is the successor to the Grace CPU used in Blackwell platforms. Together they form the Vera Rubin platform.

The flagship deployment form factor is the Vera Rubin NVL72: 72 Rubin GPUs in a single NVLink 6 domain, per rack, liquid-cooled. This is the direct successor to the GB300 NVL72 — same rack-scale approach, same liquid cooling infrastructure class, same single-NVLink-domain architecture. What changes is the generation: HBM4 memory in place of HBM3e, NVLink 6 in place of NVLink 5, and a performance step that NVIDIA targets at approximately 2× the AI training throughput of Blackwell Ultra at the same rack footprint.

HBM4 carries materially higher capacity and bandwidth than HBM3e — the exact specifications are not yet fully public, but the direction is clear: Vera Rubin continues the trajectory of more memory per GPU and faster interconnect per generation, the two variables that have defined each successive NVIDIA architecture since Volta.

03

Why procurement starts now

The procurement timeline for a platform at this stage is not measured in weeks. It is measured in relationships that already exist.

Vera Rubin allocation is at the stage where it is going to buyers who are already in standing with NVIDIA's top-tier channel and a small set of strategic customers. It is not on open order books. It is not accessible through a standard channel purchase. The people who have early access have it because they were already in the right conversations before the platform launched.

For a buyer who is not already inside that allocation process, the window to get a position that matters is now — not at commercial availability, when demand is public and lead times are already set. The lead time on a platform relationship is longer than the lead time on the hardware.

Who should be looking at Vera Rubin now:

  • Multi-year compute strategy buyers. Anyone planning infrastructure for 2027 and beyond needs to understand the roadmap they are planning against. GB300 NVL72 and Vera Rubin are a sequence, not an either/or.
  • Sovereign AI programs. National compute programs building for 2027 timelines need to be in the allocation process now. The units reserved at this stage will define what is available at launch.
  • Hyperscale operators with GB300 capacity. If you already have GB300 NVL72 reserved, understanding the Vera Rubin upgrade path — same facility, same cooling, same rack format, more performance — is a near-term planning question, not a future one.
  • GPUaaS operators positioning for the next generation. The neocloud and GPUaaS market is already having internal conversations about Vera Rubin. The operators who are not in those conversations are building a gap they will spend the next two years trying to close.
04

Facility readiness for Vera Rubin

The facility case for Vera Rubin is simpler than it appears, because the platform is in the same cooling class as GB300 NVL72.

GB300 NVL72 is already the highest-density, highest-power rack-scale liquid-cooled platform in the NVIDIA lineup. Vera Rubin NVL72 succeeds it in the same rack-scale NVL format, with the same class of liquid cooling infrastructure. A facility that has been designed and built for GB300 NVL72 has been designed and built for Vera Rubin — the infrastructure upgrade is not a new build problem.

The practical implications:

  • Liquid cooling infrastructure. High-density liquid cooling at the rack scale — the same specification as GB300 NVL72. If you have commissioned or designed for GB300, your cooling plant applies to Vera Rubin.
  • Power density. At approximately 2× the training performance of GB300 in the same rack footprint, Vera Rubin may draw higher power per rack than GB300. Exact power specifications will be confirmed at commercial availability — size to accommodate headroom above the GB300 NVL72 figure.
  • Network fabric. NVLink 6 within the rack. External network fabric requirements will follow the same NVL rack-scale architecture — plan for high-bandwidth east-west fabric between racks as with GB300.
  • Floor and structure. Same class as GB300 NVL72 rack-scale deployments. Verify specific unit weights at commercial specification confirmation.

If your facility is not yet designed for GB300 NVL72, the facility design for Vera Rubin and GB300 is the same conversation. Capacity sourcing and power procurement run alongside.

05

How the allocation process works

Vera Rubin allocation does not work the way standard hardware procurement works. There is no open purchase order channel. There is no distributor with stock. Access at this stage of the platform lifecycle is gated by relationships that exist before the order, not by willingness to pay after it.

The process:

  1. Qualifying the buyer. The channel needs to know who the compute is for, what it will be used for, and whether the buyer can take delivery at the scale they claim and fund it. Speculative buyers and unnamed end users do not get positions. Strictly end users and the organizations they formally name.
  2. Demonstrating facility readiness. The allocation channel wants to know the facility is real — not a rendering, not a lease under negotiation, but capacity that will be ready when the hardware delivers. A buyer with a powered site and a signed lease moves differently in this conversation than one planning for one.
  3. Channel introductions. Vera Rubin allocation at this stage goes through NVIDIA's top-tier channel. We hold those introductions. They do not come from an inbound enquiry to a distributor.
  4. Terms and position. Early positions are not public prices. The commercial terms at this stage — pricing, delivery windows, cancellation terms, deposit requirements — are negotiated, not quoted. Negotiating them well requires knowing what terms others are receiving and having the standing to push back.

We hold no inventory and take no position. What we provide is the introduction, the standing, and the negotiation. You contract directly with the channel partner.

The NVIDIA roadmap context

Hopper → Blackwell → Blackwell Ultra → Rubin. The cadence is accelerating.

Hopper (H100 / H200)The 2023–2025 installed-base generation. HBM3 / HBM3e, air-coolable, NVLink 4. Now entering the secondary market as operators rotate to Blackwell.
Blackwell (B200 / B300)Current-generation. HBM3e, liquid-cooled at rack scale, NVLink 5. B200 is the baseline eight-GPU system; B300 (Blackwell Ultra) adds more memory per GPU. GB300 NVL72 is the 72-GPU rack-scale form factor.
Vera Rubin (NVR100)Next generation. HBM4, NVLink 6, liquid-cooled. Vera Rubin NVL72 is the 72-GPU rack-scale successor to GB300 NVL72. Approximately 2× training performance at the same footprint.
Rubin UltraNVIDIA's expected follow-on to Vera Rubin, targeting 2027–2028. The architecture cadence that produced Hopper → Blackwell in under two years is unlikely to slow. Buyers planning five-year infrastructure strategies need to account for a generation that does not yet have a procurement path.

Questions

Asked about Vera Rubin.

01

Is Vera Rubin available now?

+

Early allocation exists, and it is heavily restricted to NVIDIA's top-tier channel and a small set of strategic customers. This is not a market where open orders exist — access requires demonstrating genuine scaled demand, financial capability and facility readiness, and being introduced through relationships that already have standing. That introduction is what we provide.

02

What is the difference between GB300 NVL72 and Vera Rubin NVL72?

+

Same rack-scale form factor — 72 GPUs in a single NVLink domain, liquid-cooled, the same infrastructure class. Vera Rubin is a full architecture generation beyond Blackwell Ultra: HBM4 memory rather than HBM3e, NVLink 6 rather than NVLink 5, and approximately twice the AI training performance at the same rack footprint. Plan for both in sequence, not as either/or.

03

Is our facility compatible with Vera Rubin?

+

If you have designed for GB300 NVL72, you have designed for Vera Rubin. The platforms are in the same cooling class — high-density liquid-cooled, same rack infrastructure approach. The upgrade path from GB300 to Vera Rubin should not require a new facility build.

04

Should we buy B300 now or wait for Vera Rubin?

+

This is a timeline and budget question, not a performance question — Vera Rubin is clearly the more capable platform. B300 delivers sooner and the cost basis is known. Vera Rubin delivers more performance but allocation is early-stage and lead times are longer. If the compute need is certain and near-term, B300. If the strategy is a 2027+ deployment and facility readiness is already planned, Vera Rubin.

05

How does TCN access Vera Rubin allocation?

+

Through relationships at the channel level that do not surface on open markets. Early allocation for a platform at this stage goes to buyers who are already in standing with the right channel partners — not to cold inbound. An advisory relationship that already has those introductions is the only practical route for a buyer who is not already inside the allocation process.

06

Is the end-user requirement strict?

+

Yes. Strictly end users and the organizations they formally name. No resellers, no intermediaries procuring for unnamed end buyers, no speculative allocation. Vera Rubin allocation at this stage is particularly scrutinised — the channel has to know who the compute is for and why.
