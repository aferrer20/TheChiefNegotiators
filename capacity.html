Density

Where high-density colocation gets difficult

Above roughly 40 kW per rack the list of operators who can genuinely deliver narrows sharply. Knowing which claims survive a site visit is most of what this desk is for.

40–70 kW per rack

Achievable in many modern facilities with contained aisles and rear-door heat exchangers. The constraint is usually cooling redundancy at full draw rather than the electrical path, and it is the question operators are least often asked.

70–150 kW per rack

Direct-to-chip liquid cooling territory. A materially shorter list of operators, and the gap between designed-for and deployed is where most of the disappointment lives. We ask what is running today, at what density, and for how long.

What we verify

  • Deliverable density in the specific hall, not the site's best case
  • Cooling headroom at your full draw with a unit down
  • Whether liquid cooling is deployed or only designed for
  • Floor loading and structural provision for the rack spec
  • Water position and any regulatory exposure in that jurisdiction

Global coverage

Power availability decides the map.

Not demand, and not the market nearest you. For latency-tolerant training work, geography is usually worth more than every clause in the contract combined.

North America

Northern Virginia, Dallas–Fort Worth and ERCOT, Phoenix, Atlanta, Chicago, Columbus, plus secondary markets that frequently beat all of them on time-to-energisation.

Nordics

Iceland, Norway and Sweden — the strongest cost-per-megawatt case in Europe for latency-tolerant training.

UK & Ireland

London and Slough for latency-bound inference; Ireland where grid position allows it.

Continental Europe

Frankfurt, Amsterdam and Paris, with Nordic and Iberian alternatives when FLAP-D power is constrained.

APAC

Latin America

Questions

Asked at the desk.

01

What rack density can you actually source?

+

Up to 150 kW per rack, with air, rear-door heat exchanger and direct-to-chip liquid cooling depending on the facility and region. Above roughly 40 kW the list of operators who can genuinely deliver narrows sharply, and above 70 kW it narrows again.

02

What does this cost the buyer?

+

Nothing directly. Winning business carries a cost of sale that exists whether or not we are involved, and that is where we are paid from. You contract directly with the operator at the rate we negotiated.

03

How quickly can capacity be identified?

+

First response within one business day, and a credible shortlist inside the first week for most requirements. Delivery is set by the operator and the utility — above a few megawatts, energisation date is usually the binding constraint rather than available space.

04

Can you help if we already have a proposal?

+

Yes, and it is a common entry point. We benchmark what you have been offered, tell you where it sits against the market, and identify which terms are actually movable. Mid-negotiation is not too late.
