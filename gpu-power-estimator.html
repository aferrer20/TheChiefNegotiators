01

Power is the filter. Everything else comes after.

Most capacity searches fail at the first question an operator asks: how many megawatts, at what density, and when. Walking in with those numbers already worked out is the difference between a screening reply and a real one.

IT loadGPU systems at published maximum draw, plus an allowance for the network fabric and storage that sit alongside them.
Facility loadIT load multiplied by PUE — what the site has to deliver from the grid or behind the meter, and what you will ultimately pay for.
Rack countHow many systems fit under the hall's density limit. Low density means more racks, more floor and longer cable runs on the fabric.
Cooling classThe heat rejection the rack demands. Above roughly 45 kW, air alone stops being a serious answer.

The figures are deliberately conservative. Real draw depends on workload, power-capping, firmware and how the operator meters it — which is exactly why the contracted power definition is a term worth negotiating. For the terms themselves, see GPUaaS contract negotiation and the GPU contract terms that move the price.

If the density is the constraint rather than the megawatts, the answer is not always a new lease. Two-phase cooling can lift density inside a footprint you already pay for. When the megawatts are the constraint, start with power procurement and capacity sourcing.

Questions

Asked about the estimate.

01

How much power does a GB300 NVL72 rack need?

+

Published figures put a GB300 NVL72 rack at roughly 120 kW nominal, with OEM specifications up to about 140 kW. The estimator uses 135 kW per rack for planning. It is liquid-cooled by design and needs a coolant distribution unit and facility water, so an air-cooled hall cannot host it at any price.

02

How many racks does a 1,024-GPU B300 cluster need?

+

It depends on the density the facility can carry. An HGX B300 system draws about 14.5 kW, so at 50 kW per rack you fit three systems, or 24 GPUs, per rack — around 43 compute racks for 1,024 GPUs, before network and storage. At 130 kW per rack that falls to 16. Set the density in the estimator to see the trade-off.

03

What PUE should I assume?

+

Modern liquid-cooled AI facilities commonly design for a PUE between about 1.1 and 1.3; older air-cooled halls run higher. The estimator defaults to 1.30 as a conservative planning figure. The operator's contracted PUE, and how it is metered and billed, is a term worth negotiating in its own right.

04

Is this a quote?

+

No. It is planning arithmetic from published system power figures. Real draw depends on workload, configuration, firmware power limits and the facility design. It tells you the order of magnitude of what you are looking for — a conversation gets you capacity, allocation and terms against your specific requirement.

05

No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser. Nothing leaves the page unless you choose to email us the requirement.
