01

What H100 is, and why it matters now

NVIDIA H100 is the Hopper architecture GPU that defined the 2023–2025 AI infrastructure build cycle. Two variants shipped at scale: the H100 SXM5 with 80 GB of HBM3 and the full NVLink fabric, used in DGX H100 and HGX H100 training systems; and the H100 PCIe with 80 GB of HBM2e, used in standard rack servers for inference and mixed workloads.

The case for H100 today is not architecture — it is installed base and secondary market. More H100 systems have shipped than any other AI GPU in history. As operators rotate those fleets to Blackwell, a large volume of H100 hardware is coming off original deployments with substantial operational life remaining. Buyers who need proven capacity without a Blackwell facility, or who need it faster than a new allocation can deliver it, find H100 is often the most practical path.

The proven software stack is part of it: CUDA, PyTorch, TensorFlow, NCCL and every major training and inference framework has been optimised on H100 for years. Teams already on Hopper move workloads across without re-engineering.

It does not fit a buyer starting a multi-year build, or one who needs more than 80 GB per GPU. That is current-generation Blackwell — see H200 for the memory upgrade within Hopper, or B200 for the next architecture.

03

Where H100 allocation comes from

The secondary market for H100 is the largest in the GPU sector by installed-base volume.

  • Fleet rotation. Operators who took H100 at scale in 2023–2024 are rotating to Blackwell. Those fleets come off original deployments with life remaining — the holder wants a clean sale rather than a public listing.
  • Cancelled and restructured orders. Committed orders that no longer fit a changed roadmap, a financing event that shifted the timeline, or a site that cannot energise on the original schedule.
  • Channel positions. Allocation held by OEM channel partners and integrators that has never been advertised, and is reached by already being in those relationships.
  • Data center operator disposals. Large operators building to new platforms sometimes have H100 systems to move that are not part of their commercial offer — they are a distraction to manage internally, and a discreet introduction is what they want.

We hold none of it and we are not the seller. What we provide is the introduction to the holder and the negotiation once you are talking. The broader picture is on the GPU cluster procurement mandate.

04

What the facility has to carry

  • Power — SXM5. About 10.2 kW per DGX H100 system. Four systems in a rack is around 40 kW. A 512-GPU cluster of 64 systems is roughly 650 kW before networking and storage. The power estimator works it through for your count.
  • Power — PCIe. Significantly lower: a server with eight H100 PCIe GPUs typically draws under 5 kW. PCIe fits facilities that SXM5 density would overwhelm.
  • Cooling. Both variants are air-coolable within the density the hall can support. Most enterprise halls manage H100 without a cooling upgrade at moderate rack count — unlike Blackwell liquid-cooled platforms, H100 does not require a new cooling infrastructure commitment.
  • Fabric. InfiniBand HDR or NDR has been the standard compute fabric for Hopper training clusters. RoCE Ethernet is common for inference. Reallocated clusters sometimes include their fabric and sometimes do not. Establishing this before price negotiation matters.
  • Floor. Standard data center floor loading is generally sufficient. Verify the delivery path and rack weight with the specific hardware before signing.

If the servers need a home, capacity sourcing and power procurement run alongside.

05

The terms that matter on reallocated H100

Reallocated hardware at H100 volumes is a mature market. It also carries risks that have to be priced rather than assumed away.

  1. Provenance and title. A clear chain of ownership, a seller entitled to sell, and no outstanding financing or lien against the hardware. H100 systems were financed in large numbers in 2023 — the paperwork matters.
  2. Warranty and support. Whether remaining OEM warranty transfers, what enterprise support is available after it ends, and who answers when a GPU fails in month six.
  3. Condition and acceptance. Operating history, logged failure events, and an acceptance test on delivery with a remedy for units that fail it.
  4. Configuration completeness. NICs, cables, optics, InfiniBand switches and storage — included, excluded, or assumed. The paper should match what the listing implies.
  5. Export and end-use compliance. H100 is export-controlled hardware. Seller and buyer both need documentation correct for the destination and the declared end use.
  6. Software licensing. Enterprise software subscriptions attached to the original purchase may not transfer. Price the replacement before signing.
06

H100 vs H200 vs B200 — the decision matrix

We have no reason to prefer any of them. We do not sell them.

Choose H100 when80 GB per GPU fits the workload, cost is the primary variable, the facility is air-cooled, and the team already runs on Hopper. Often significantly cheaper than new allocation through reallocation.
Choose H200 when80 GB per GPU is not enough — H200's 141 GB HBM3e is the upgrade within Hopper, still air-coolable, with the same facility profile as H100 SXM5. Fits models too large for H100 without a Blackwell build.
Choose B200 / B300 whenYou are building for a multi-year horizon, want reserved capacity or GPUaaS rather than owned hardware, or need the performance density only Blackwell delivers. Liquid-cooled — from 512 GPUs or 1 MW on B200, B300 or GB300 NVL72.

03

H100 SXM5 or PCIe — which should we buy?

+

SXM5 for training clusters: higher memory bandwidth, full NVLink at 900 GB/s per GPU, required for large-scale distributed training. PCIe for inference nodes and mixed-use racks: 350 W per GPU, fits any PCIe server without a custom baseboard, and widely available through reallocation. The decision is workload and facility first.

04

Can H100 run in our existing air-cooled hall?

+

Yes, subject to rack power density. SXM5 runs at about 700 W per GPU — a DGX H100 draws roughly 10.2 kW. PCIe is easier at about 350 W per GPU. Most enterprise air-cooled halls can take H100 at moderate rack density without a cooling upgrade. Verify deliverable kilowatts per rack before agreeing a configuration.

05

Should we buy H100 or H200?

+

If memory is the constraint — your model does not fit in 80 GB per GPU — H200's 141 GB HBM3e is the clear answer. If 80 GB is sufficient and cost is the variable, H100 is often significantly cheaper through reallocation. The software stack is identical; the performance difference is mainly memory capacity and bandwidth.
