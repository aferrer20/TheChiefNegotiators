Scale

Four bands, and the constraint changes at each one.

What is hard about 512 GPUs is nothing like what is hard about 50,000. Find your band — the advisory work is different in kind, not just in size. The 12 to 14 week lead time holds across all four; what changes is the power and siting work behind it.

Band 01Pilot512 – 1,024 GPUs

Proving a workload before committing capital. Short term, on-fleet, fast. We work these only where production scale is credibly behind them.

Band 02Production1,024 – 4,096 GPUs

The band where contract terms start costing real money — utilisation floors, burst rights, egress, refresh clauses. Most of the value here is in the paper, not the price.

Band 03Frontier4,096 – 25,000 GPUs

Fabric topology, storage tier and power density become the specification. Fewer than twenty operators globally can hold this, and we know which ones can prove it.

Band 04Hyperscale25,000 GPUs · 50 MW+

No longer a compute purchase. It is a power, siting and delivery-schedule problem with GPUs attached — assembled as one transaction.

For hyperscalers, frontier labs and sovereign programmes

Above 50 MW, GPUs stop being the constraint. Power and schedule are.

At this scale the deal is not one purchase, it is three that have to arrive together: an energised site with the density and cooling to take 120 kW racks, allocation that lands on the schedule the site can hold, and a term structure that survives a platform refresh mid-contract. Normally that is three searches run by three teams who never meet. We hold relationships at every layer — operator, developer, utility, OEM channel and offtaker — so it can be assembled as one transaction, negotiated once, and closed without your position circulating a market that talks. Anonymous at first contact, MNDA before anything specific, and nothing about your requirement leaves the room.

Direct Answers

Asked most often.

The first two are the ones that decide whether we are useful to you at all.

01

Do you work with brokers, resellers or intermediaries?

+

No. This desk is open to the end user — the party whose workload runs on the hardware and whose balance sheet stands behind the contract — or an advisor that party names in writing.

We do not work with brokers assembling a position to resell, and we do not join chains where the same allocation has been shopped through four parties before it reaches us. The principled reason is that it is not what we do. The practical reason is that operators will not quote into a chain, so nobody in it ever sees a real number.

If you are an intermediary with a genuine named end user behind you, that is a different conversation and we will have it.

02

Can you send a GPU rate card?

+

No. We are not a supplier — we do not buy, sell or hold anything, so there is no rate card of ours to send.

Any rate card you have been sent from elsewhere is close to worthless anyway. Price on a cluster moves with scale, term, prepayment, power cost at the site, cooling type, network fabric and how badly the operator needs an anchor that quarter. The only number that matters is the one a partner will sign against your requirement.

We could not send one if we wanted to. We hold no inventory and set no prices — every number comes from the operator or the channel partner, direct to you. What we do is get your requirement to the partners whose position fits it, tell you where their response sits against comparable deals we have seen, and negotiate the terms around it.

03

What is the smallest cluster you will work on?

+

512 GPUs, and only with a credible path to production scale behind it. Below that the economics do not support advisory involvement on either side, and you are genuinely better off on public cloud on-demand until the workload is proven.

04

Can you actually deliver at hyperscale?

+

Above roughly 50 MW the binding constraint stops being GPUs and becomes energised power, delivery schedule, and cooling capable of rack densities over 120 kW.

We work that layer directly — powered shell and build-to-suit, interconnection position, PPAs and behind-the-meter options, liquid cooling retrofit against live halls — and assemble compute, capacity and power into one transaction instead of three separate searches on three timelines. That is the specific case for using us at this scale.

05

Should we buy hardware or contract GPUaaS?

+

It turns on utilisation and refresh exposure.

Sustained utilisation above roughly seventy per cent on a workload you can forecast three years out favours owning — if you can secure the power and the hall, which is the part most buyers underestimate. Spiky or uncertain demand, or a workload that will migrate to the next platform inside eighteen months, favours a reserved GPUaaS commitment where refresh risk sits with the operator.

We model both against your actual load profile before recommending either, and we are paid the same way regardless of which you pick.

06

What is the lead time?

+

On GPUaaS and reserved clusters: first response within one business day, and 12 to 14 weeks from executed contract to service across B200, B300 and GB300 NVL72. Where the site and power position are already in hand that holds firm; where a hall still needs building, siting and energisation set the schedule rather than silicon. Hardware allocation moves on its own clock — it depends entirely on what has surfaced, so we tell you what is reachable rather than quoting a standard window.

07

What does this cost us?

+

Nothing directly on a buy-side mandate. Winning business carries a cost of sale that exists whether or not we are involved, and that is where we are paid from — so our involvement is funded by the transaction rather than added to it. We hold no inventory and take no position, so nothing we bring forward is something we need you to take.
